# NIGHTSHADE — LORE

**Status:** a working document for whoever writes the next line of text in this game. Not a wiki.
There is no map of ancient kingdoms in here and there will not be one.

**Companion:** `docs/design/MAGIC_KINETIC.md`. That file has the numbers; this one has the reasons.
They are meant to be edited together. **A multiplier with no reason here is a multiplier the player
will experience as a bug.**

**Outranked by:** `docs/design/DIRECTION.md`, which is the project owner speaking. Three sections here
exist because of it — **§4.2** (what a spell is), **§6a** (the city and the two kinds of gun), and the
register note in **§10.7**.

---

## 0. HOW TO USE THIS FILE

Read §1 through §4 once. They are twelve short paragraphs and they are the whole cosmology; every
mechanic in the game falls out of them. After that, skip to **§11 — the fixed facts and the open
questions.** Eight things you may not contradict, fourteen things that are yours.

Then write. §12 has the formats — an NPC's daily line, a caravan note, an Almanac entry — with real
examples you can copy the shape of.

**Three things this file is deliberately not:**

- **Not a timeline.** There are no dates. Nobody in Ember Hollow keeps a calendar that goes back past
  their own grandmother, and the game is better for it.
- **Not a map.** The world is procedurally generated and infinite. Nothing in the fiction may depend on
  a place existing at a particular coordinate, except the town, which is at the origin.
- **Not finished.** It is a spine with room on it. If you find a gap, that is a place to write, not an
  error to report.

## 1. THE ONE AXIOM

> **Light is a substance.**

Not a metaphor. Not a wave. A **thing** — you can store it, spend it, carry it, run out of it, spill
it, and have it taken off you. It collects in living creatures the way warmth collects in a stone in
the sun. It can be rendered out of plants, which is what lamp oil is. It can be compressed until it
sits in your palm as a coal, which is what **Ember** is.

Everything else in this document is a consequence of that one sentence, and so is every mechanic in
the game:

- A lamp is a **container**, so it can be full or empty, and a lamplighter carries a tank.
- Light can be **stolen**, so there is something that steals it.
- Stolen light is still light, so it can be **called back** — which is why a lantern is a weapon.
- A creature that has been drained is not dead. It is **empty**, and it keeps walking.
- Light that has cooled is **portable**, which is why the dead drop currency and why you can spend it.

Hold onto the ordinariness of it. In this world light is *plumbing*. It is a municipal utility. People
complained about the rates.

## 2. WHAT WENT OUT

*"The lamps went out on a Tuesday."*

Not the sun. The sun is fine. It comes up every sixteen minutes and it is beautiful.

What went out was **the Wick** — one great lantern, a long way down, in a room under the world, tended
in shifts for a very long time by people who called themselves lamplighters and who mostly thought of
themselves as employees. It was a *job*. There were rotas. There was a form you filled in when the oil
delivery was late. Somewhere in Ember Hollow there is a ledger with four hundred years of signatures in
it and the last page is half full.

Nobody knows why it went out. The three theories, all of which you will hear in town, all of which are
allowed to stay unresolved:

1. **The oil ran out.** The most boring answer and the most popular one. Grandfather Wick believes this
   and refuses to elaborate.
2. **Somebody let it.** Mabel Thorn believes this, and will not say who she suspects, and gets short
   with you if you push.
3. **It got tired.** Pip believes this. Pip is nine.

**Do not resolve it in v1.** See §10.

What is certain is that it happened on a Tuesday, in the afternoon, and that the light did not go out
everywhere at once — it went out **the way a town goes dark in a power cut**, in patches, some streets
before others, and there are places that have not gone dark yet and places that went dark first and
worst. That patchiness *is* the shade-tier gradient. It is not a curse spreading outward. It is a grid
failing unevenly.

The tone of the apocalypse is the most important thing in this document. **It was a maintenance
failure.** Nobody prophesied it. Nobody was chosen. There is no dark lord; there is an unpaid invoice
and a room nobody has been down to in a while. That is both funnier and sadder than a prophecy, and it
is why the town works.

## 3. THE NIGHTSHADE

The dark was always there. It was simply *pushed against*, everywhere, all the time, by the Wick and by
several thousand smaller lamps, and a thing that is pushed against constantly does not develop
opinions.

When the pushing stopped, the dark found that it could move, and then that it could **want**.

It wants light. Specifically it wants light that has been **gathered** — a lamp, a hearth, a living
creature, a person carrying two hundred Ember in a satchel. Loose daylight is nothing to it; it is
diffuse and thin and everywhere and it cannot be *drunk*. But a lit lantern in a dark field is a
concentration. It is a meal.

This is why the game's first instruction is a warning. When you reach the lantern post, the prompt says
`HOLD [E] — LIGHT THE LANTERN`, and under it, small: *"It's going to get their attention."* That is
literally true and it is the central bargain of the whole game. **Every light you make is a light you
must then defend, and that is not a cruel design decision, it is what the fiction says.**

The dark is not evil. It is not a god and it has no plan. Call it a **pressure**. It is what happens to
a room when you stop paying the bill.

Nobody in Ember Hollow calls it "the Nightshade" out loud, incidentally. Connie Vane does, in writing,
because she is a cartographer and needs a label for the legend. Everyone else says **"the dark"**, or
"it", or nothing at all and just looks out the window.

## 4. WHY LIGHT HURTS SOME THINGS AND NOT OTHERS

This is the section that has to be right, because it is the section the player will *feel* in the first
ten minutes. Three sentences.

> **The unbodied are stolen light held in a shape. Shining light on them is not burning them — it is
> calling it back. It hurts them the way opening your hand hurts a fist.**

A Wisp is not a creature that eats light. A Wisp is *light that has been eaten*, held in a knot by the
dark around it, and it has no body of its own at all. So when you open your lantern's shutter on one,
what happens is not combustion; it is **release**. The knot comes undone. The light goes where light
goes. This is why a Wisp killed by the lamp **chimes all the way to the end of its note**, and a Wisp
killed by a bullet **stops mid-note** — a bullet punches a hole in a knot and the rest of it just
falls apart, unfinished. Two sounds. Same enemy. That is the whole system in an audio cue and it is
free.

> **A Husk's body is matter. The dark in it is a tenant, not the structure. You cannot evict a tenant
> by lighting the house.**

A Husk is a person — or a dog, or a horse, or, at least once, another lamplighter — whose gathered light
was drunk out of them. The dark did not build the body. The body was already there. It is walking
because there is something in it that wants to keep walking, and shining a lamp at it annoys the
tenant and does nothing whatsoever to the walls.

So you take the house down. This is not a satisfying answer and it is not meant to be. **This is the
game's grief**, and the whole reason the shooting has weight: a Husk on a road is walking the route it
walked. It goes to the gate at the hour it always went to the gate. Ask Connie about the Husk on the
eastern track sometime; she has mapped its circuit twice and she will not go and look at its face.

> **And a few things are both.**

A **Spitter** has learned to carry the dark in a sac and throw it, and the sac is full of light it has
not finished digesting — which is why it *glows* for a second and a half before it throws, and why
hitting that glow with a lamp makes the whole thing come apart. A **Snuffer** has wrapped itself in a
shell of dark; peel that off with light and there is an ordinary, frightened, entirely physical body
underneath that dies to bullets like anything else. A **Warden**'s plate is honest matter and light
simply goes *around* it, because that is what light does with an obstacle, and finds the seams.

**The rule for a writer:** if you invent a creature, decide first whether it is *made of* light, or
*holding* light, or *neither*. Everything else — how it dies, what it sounds like, whether it has a
shadow — follows. If you cannot answer that question about your creature, you have not invented it yet.

### 4.1 The tell about shadows

The unbodied **do not cast a shadow. They cast a pool.**

This is not a stylistic choice, it is the axiom being consistent: a thing made of light standing on the
ground makes the ground brighter. It is also the single most useful fact in the game, because you can
see it at sixty metres when you cannot see anything else, and every player works it out for themselves
without being told. Look at the ground. Dark patch, use the gun. Bright patch, use the lamp.

Nobody in the fiction will ever explain this to you. Mabel knows it. Mabel assumes you know it.

### 4.2 What a spell is

There is no magic in this world. There is a **trade**.

A spell is a *technique* — a way of spending light out of a lamp on purpose, in a shape, quickly. It is
closer to welding than to sorcery. The lamplighters worked out most of them the way any trade works
things out: somebody needed to get a stuck shutter open in a hurry, somebody else needed to not be
seen, and a hundred years later there is a name for it and an apprentice learns it in a fortnight.

**This is why the lantern is the focus and there is no wand.** The lamp is the tool. A spell is a thing
you know how to do *with* the tool, and you learn it from a person or from a manual, both of which are
plausible things to find in a caravan wreck.

Four consequences that keep the fiction honest:

- **Nobody gestures.** You open a shutter, you angle a reflector, you crack the housing on purpose.
  Every spell is a thing you do to a lamp with your hands.
- **A spell costs oil, because oil is what light is made of.** There is no separate mystical resource
  and there never will be. Running out means running out.
- **The fire blast is not light.** The Emberlance vents burning oil. It hurts *bodies* — which is exactly
  why it is mechanically kinetic (`MAGIC_KINETIC.md` §1.1) — and it is the one technique that a
  lamplighter's guild would have been embarrassed about, because it wastes fuel and sets things alight.
  Mabel thinks it is vulgar. Mabel will sell you one.
- **Healing works because you are a container too.** Mending is putting some of the lamp's light back
  into the person holding it. That is also, quietly, the most upsetting fact in this document: it means
  a Husk is a person somebody could have topped up in time.

## 5. THE THINGS OUT THERE

Written as the Almanac would have them, which is to say: written by Connie Vane, who has seen four of
these in person and is not saying which four.

**WISP** — *"A chime with an opinion."* The commonest. A knot of stolen light the size of a fist,
moving as though it has just remembered something. Harmless in ones. In sixes it is how people die
three metres from their own door. The chime is the light in it, ringing.

**MOTE** — *"Slow. Patient. Behind you."* Larger, dimmer, drifts. Motes do not hunt; they *arrive*. If
you have ever turned round and found one at your shoulder you already understand every design decision
in this document.

**GLOAM** — *"The hole in the evening."* Not a knot of light — a knot of *appetite*, and the only thing
out there that eats light faster than it drinks it. A Gloam does not have to touch you to hurt you: the
world simply closes in, your lamp gets smaller, and the horizon comes to within twelve paces. Bullets
go through it and nothing happens, which is the most frightening sentence in the Almanac. Mabel's
advice, verbatim: *"Don't shoot it, sweetheart. Show it the lamp. It'll leave."*

**HUSK** — *"Somebody's errand, still being run."* See §4. There are two entries in the Almanac for
Husks: the general one and a second one, in different handwriting, that just gives a name and a date
and says *known route: the eastern track, gate to mill, dusk*. Nobody has told Pip which entry is which.

**SPITTER** — *"It shows you before it does it."* A body that has learned a trick, carrying the dark in
a sac under the jaw. The glow before the throw is the sac working. It has never occurred to a Spitter
that the glow tells you anything.

**SNUFFER** — *"A candle-cap, walking."* Named for the little bell-shaped tin on a stick that you put
over a flame to end it politely. Something wrapped itself in dark until it had no shape at all, and it
goes about the business of ending lights. Under the shell: a person, or what is left of the shape of
one. **Some players will find that worse than the Bulwark and they are right to.**

**WARDEN** — *"It has been told to stand there."* Plated at the front, unbothered, advancing. Whatever
it was, it was in a uniform.

**BULWARK** — *"The reason there are three waves and not two."* Pip claims to have seen one up close.
Pip says it looked at him and he said nothing, because he is brave. Pip has never left the walls.

## 6. THE MATERIALS

Five substances carry the entire economy and every one of them is the axiom in a different state.

**EMBER** — light that has cooled enough to carry. It comes off anything that dies with light still in
it, which is everything, which is why it is the currency. Warm to hold. Cools to nothing in about a
week if you leave it in a drawer, so **nobody in Ember Hollow saves money**, which explains a great
deal about the town's architecture.

**ASHROOT** — a plant that gathers light the way other plants gather water, and holds it in its sap. It
grows in beds. You plant it at dusk because that is when it is hungriest. **Rendered down, its sap is
lamp oil** — which means the beds behind Odo's inn are, in the most literal possible sense, an
ammunition factory, and that the cosiest thing in the game feeds the loudest. Nobody in town finds this
remarkable. It is just what a garden is for.

**ASH** (the mineral, not the tree) — what a thing leaves when the light has been taken out of it
completely. Grey, light, useless alone, necessary for everything.

**IRONWOOD, GLASS, COLDIRON** — ordinary industry. Somebody has to make lantern housings. Somebody
does. Mabel's shop has a bill of materials pinned to the wall in a hand that is not hers.

**SHADEGLASS** — dark that has been compressed until it is matter. The only substance in the world that
is both things at once, which is why it is worth what it is worth, why it only exists where the dark
has been thickest longest, and why every good thing in the endgame is made of it. **It is cold and it
does not reflect anything**, and Mabel will handle it but she puts her gloves on first, and does not
explain.

## 6a. THE CITY, AND THE TWO KINDS OF GUN

`DIRECTION.md` §3 is the owner's, and it is a gift to the fiction:

> *"a sub machine gun and assault rifle are still cool potential weapons but youd have to have like a
> well lit city manufacturing them and the ammunition"*

That is exactly right and it follows from the axiom without being forced. **A factory needs light.** Not
metaphorically — a machine shop cutting rifling needs to *see*, all night, every night, and the only
thing that makes light in this world is a lamp with oil in it, and oil comes from beds of a plant that
has to be grown somewhere safe. **A precision rifle is downstream of a well-lit farm.**

So there are two kinds of gun and you can tell them apart by looking.

**VILLAGE WORK** — a bolt-action rifle, a revolver, a pump shotgun, a length of pipe with a trigger
group somebody was proud of. Made by a person, in a shed, with files. **Forgiving on ammunition**,
because the ammunition is also made in the shed and the tolerances are generous enough that a slightly
wrong cartridge still goes off. Every one of them is slightly different. Mabel can name the maker of
most of them at a glance and has opinions about four of those makers.

**CITY WORK** — an assault rifle, a submachine gun, a machine gun. Made on a line, with power, to a
drawing. **Ammunition-hungry** in a way that is not a balance decision but a fact about the thing:
precision-made weapons want precision-made cartridges, and there is exactly one place that makes those,
and it only makes them when its lights are on.

> **Ammunition is the real currency, not the gun.**

This is the sentence that makes the whole economy make sense, and it is `DIRECTION.md`'s. Nobody guards
rifles. There are rifles in every wreck between here and the Fen. What people guard, and hoard, and
ration, and count twice, is **brass**. A man will lend you his rifle and not lend you six rounds for it,
and he is not being unkind — the rifle can be replaced by a shed and the rounds cannot be replaced by
anything but a lit city.

### How the grid works, in-world

The old lamplighters' network was a **grid**, in the ordinary municipal sense: lanterns on posts, close
enough together to hand light along, all of it fed from the Wick. When you light a Lantern Post you are
not making a new thing, you are **reconnecting a fitting**. Two lanterns close enough to see each other
form a **Wick Line** and the road between them carries. Enough Wick Lines and a whole district comes
back, and if the district that comes back happens to contain a machine shop, then there is brass in the
shop again next week.

**A Pall is a fault.** That is all it is, and everyone in the trade would recognise it: a stretch where
the dark got in and got comfortable, and nothing you hang there will stay lit until it is pushed back.
You do not clear a Pall by killing anything. You clear it by putting light in it and keeping it there,
which is a job, which is the job.

**So the causal chain the player lives inside is:** do the rounds → the grid reconnects → the city's
lights come on → there is ammunition in the shop → you can afford to be somewhere worse next week. The
player is not *becoming powerful*. **They are restoring a utility, and the utility is arming them.** That
is a better fantasy than a skill tree and it is the same fantasy as the town getting nicer.

Nobody in Ember Hollow is romantic about this. Connie has a chart.

## 7. EMBER HOLLOW

**The town is not a fortress. It is a lamp with houses around it.**

That is the whole design of it. There is a Great Lantern in the middle — the Wick's much smaller,
much-loved cousin — and everything else grew outward from the circle of light it makes, at whatever
angle happened to be convenient. The wall came later and it is not a very good wall. It has never been
tested.

**The town is always safe. Forever.** This is a promise the game makes and never breaks (GDD §9.8), and
it is lore-backed rather than hand-waved: the Great Lantern has never once gone out, because
**Grandfather Wick has never once gone properly to bed.** He dozes. He dozes *near it*. He has dozed
near it for longer than anyone can account for. That is the reason the town is safe, and it is a person,
not a wall, which is the correct answer for this game.

Every lantern you light out in the world is **a little Ember Hollow**: a save, an ammunition top-up, a
respawn, a bed, a refuelled lamp. The town is simply the first lantern, upgraded, with people who came
and stayed. When two of your lanterns are close enough to see each other they make a **Wick Line** and
the road between them is safe, and that is not a game mechanic dressed as fiction — that is how the old
grid worked, and if you look at the ground on a Wick Line you can sometimes see the original paving.

You are not rebuilding the grid. You are **doing the rounds.** That is the actual job. It always was.

## 8. THE FIVE PEOPLE

They already exist (GDD §5) and they are good. Do not add a sixth without a reason; add *lines*.

The rule for all five: **they are small, they are specific, and none of them is a quest-giver.** Nobody
in Ember Hollow will ever tell you your destiny. Two of them will tell you about the weather.

**MABEL THORN** — gunsmith, ex-lamplighter, two fingers short on the left hand.
> *"Sweetheart, I've buried better guns than this. Give it here and I'll make it worse in a way you'll
> like."*

She took the fingers off herself, with the tool that was nearest, to get out of a Husk's hand. The Husk
was her brother. **She tells you this exactly once** — when the second wing of the Almanac museum opens
— and never mentions it again, and if you ask, she talks about the weather. She is the only person in
town who knows what the eastern track Husk's name was, and she is the one who wrote the second Husk
entry in the Almanac, and she has never gone to look.

Do not soften her and do not give her a second scene about it. **One scene. Then the weather.**

**ODO** — the innkeeper. Round, anxious, vaguely moth-shaped. Keeps soup warm past midnight for someone
who might not come back.
> *"I kept a bowl. I always keep a bowl. It's not — I'm not waiting up, it's just that soup keeps."*

Odo is the emotional load-bearing wall of the entire game. Everything he says is about food and none of
it is about food. He owns the Ashroot beds and does not think of himself as an arms manufacturer.

**CONSTANCE "CONNIE" VANE** — cartographer, runs the board, is *thrilled* by danger she has personally
never once attended.
> *"Tier four! Oh, that's marvellous. If my knee were better I'd be right behind you. Two hundred
> metres behind you."*

Connie writes the Almanac. Every enemy entry in §5 is in her voice. She has mapped the eastern track
Husk's circuit twice, precisely, and has never once drawn its face, and if you notice that out loud she
changes the subject with real speed.

**PIP** — a kid. Collects your finds, trades charms, is a confident liar.
> *"I've seen a Bulwark. Up close. It looked at me and I said nothing, because I am brave."*

Pip's lies get *more* specific the less true they are. This is the entire joke and it does not need
help. Pip has never been outside the wall. Pip will ask you to take him, once, and you cannot, and
neither of you mentions it again.

**GRANDFATHER WICK** — the old lantern-keeper. Mostly asleep. Upgrades the Great Lantern. Speaks in
aphorisms he refuses to explain.
> *"A lamp doesn't argue with the dark. It just declines to be part of it. Mm. Off you go."*

He is the oldest lamplighter and he has been down. **He knows what is at the bottom and he is not
going to tell you**, and every time you get close to asking he is asleep, and you are never entirely
sure whether that is convenient for him. He is not being mysterious to be mysterious. He simply thinks
you will find out at the appropriate point in the rota.

## 9. THE DEEPSHADE

The Deepshade is not a biome that happens to be dark. **It is the room the Wick is in**, and the
approach to it, and the parts of the old grid that failed first and hardest, and they failed so long
ago that the dark down there has had time to become *furniture*. That is where Shadeglass comes from.
That is why there is no sky.

The Lv 30 gate is a **lamplighter's key**. Not a magic key. A brass key, with a number stamped on it,
of a pattern that unlocks maintenance access, and there were once four hundred of them and now there
are very few. Getting one should feel less like being chosen and more like **being given a set of keys
on your first day.**

The endgame is going down to see whether it can be relit.

> ## **DO NOT ANSWER THAT QUESTION IN V1.**

Not "it cannot be relit". Not "it can". The player reaches the door, and the door is real, and what is
behind it is v2's problem. The correct v1 ending is **arriving** — standing in a room with the largest
dark object in the world in it, holding a lamp, at the end of a very long walk, with the town still lit
behind you because you did the rounds. That is an ending. It does not need a boss.

If you find yourself writing the answer, stop and write another one of Odo's soup lines instead. It will
do the game more good.

## 10. REGISTER — how this world talks

Six rules. They are the difference between this game's tone and the tone of every other game with
lanterns in it.

1. **Civic, not epic.** Rotas, invoices, ledgers, forms, the rates, the rounds. The apocalypse had
   paperwork. Nobody says "realm", "ancient", "forsaken", or "the prophecy".
2. **No capital-letter Evil.** The dark is a pressure and a bill. It does not gloat, it has no name for
   itself, and it never speaks. **Nothing hostile in this game ever says a word.**
3. **Understatement carries the grief.** The saddest line in the game is *"I kept a bowl."* Nobody weeps
   on screen. Sorrow arrives sideways, in what people talk about instead.
4. **Short sentences and ordinary words.** If a line needs a semicolon it needs a rewrite. These people
   are not writers, except Connie, who is bad at it in an endearing way.
5. **Warm is not the same as soft.** Ember Hollow is kind and it is also a place where a woman took her
   own fingers off and then went back to work. Do not sand that off. The town is worth coming home to
   *because* it costs something to keep.
6. **Nobody explains the mechanics.** No NPC has ever said "radiant damage". Mabel says *"show it the
   lamp"*. That is the ceiling on how technical anyone gets, forever.
7. **No modern-military register, anywhere.** `DIRECTION.md` §1: the FPS *feel* stays, the CoD *fiction*
   goes. Nobody says "contact", "tango", "oscar-mike", "hostiles", "eyes on", "breach", or "operator".
   These are lamplighters, farmers and a woman with a shed full of files. When Mabel talks about a gun
   she talks about it like a tool she has repaired, because it is. **Guns in this world are agricultural
   equipment that got a promotion.**

## 11. FIXED FACTS AND OPEN QUESTIONS

### 11.1 The eight you may not contradict

1. **Light is a substance** (§1). Everything else is downstream and nothing may break it.
2. **What failed was one lantern, a long way down, and it failed as a maintenance failure** (§2). Not a
   war, not a curse, not a betrayal by a named villain.
3. **The unbodied are stolen light; the bodied are matter with a tenant** (§4). This is the affinity
   system and it is not negotiable, because it is arithmetic in another file.
4. **Ember Hollow is never attacked and never falls. Ever.** (§7, GDD §9.8.) No dream sequence, no
   flash-forward, no "what if" — the promise is only worth something if it is never even threatened as
   a *possibility*.
5. **Nothing hostile speaks** (§10.2).
6. **Whether the Wick can be relit is unanswered in v1** (§9).
7. **Magic is a trade, not a mystery** (§4.2). No wands, no gestures, no separate mystical resource, and
   the lantern is always the tool. This one comes from `DIRECTION.md` §2 and it is load-bearing for the
   spell school in `MAGIC_KINETIC.md` §1.9.
8. **A factory needs light, and ammunition is the currency** (§6a). From `DIRECTION.md` §3. Nothing may
   imply that precision weapons or their cartridges can be made in the dark.

### 11.2 The fourteen that are yours

1. **Which theory about the Wick going out is true**, or whether the truth is a fourth thing, or nothing.
2. **Who else was on the rota.** There were four hundred keys. Where did the other lamplighters go?
   Some of them are Husks and you can meet them. Some of them founded towns you have not found.
3. **What the caravans were carrying**, who sent them, and whether anyone is still expecting them. The
   wrecks are 1 per 10 chunks and each has a note in it: that is a *lot* of note-shaped space and it is
   the best worldbuilding real estate in the game.
4. **Whether the Groves are natural.** They are guaranteed safe, even at night, and nobody knows why.
   Something is keeping them lit. Do not necessarily say what.
5. **The Standing Stones.** They predate the grid. They work with it anyway. Nobody knows who put them
   there and Connie has a theory she is embarrassed about.
6. **What the critters are.** Sixty Almanac entries of small living things that are still out there when
   nothing else is. Why are *they* fine?
7. **What Grandfather Wick saw down there**, and whether he is asleep or hiding.
8. **The eastern track Husk's name.** Mabel knows. You may decide whether the player ever learns it, and
   the correct answer is probably no.
9. **Whether Pip gets to go outside.** Ever. This is a v2-sized decision and it should be earned.
10. **What the town looked like before**, findable only in the ledger, the paving under the Wick Lines,
    and one building whose door is on the wrong side because the road used to go the other way.
11. **Seasons and weather as fiction, not just as an art variant.** Snow in winter — does the dark move
    differently in the cold? Nobody has written this down yet and there is a whole quiet system in it.
12. **What a Wickmark actually is.** Mechanically it is prestige and a flame colour past level 30. In
    the fiction it is presumably a mark, on something, meaning something, and that is unwritten.
13. **The city.** `DIRECTION.md` §3 requires one and §6a explains why it must be lit. Nothing else about
    it is decided: its name, how far it is, who runs it, whether it resents the villages, whether
    anybody there has been to Ember Hollow. **This is the largest piece of unwritten ground in the
    fiction and the most valuable.** Note the constraint from §0: nothing may depend on it being at a
    particular coordinate.
14. **Who taught you the first spell.** You know one technique before the game starts, because you are
    on the rota. Somebody showed you. They are not in the game.

## 12. FORMATS — copy the shape

### 12.1 NPC daily line

One new line per NPC per in-game day (GDD §5.7). ≤ 90 characters so it fits the HUD plate at 320×240
without wrapping twice. **Never about a quest. Never about the player's stats.** About the weather,
food, a small worry, or a thing they noticed.

```
ODO      "The beds took badly this week. Too dry. Everything's too dry lately."
ODO      "You've got mud on you. That's fine. That's — that's what floors are for."
MABEL    "Wind's from the east. Sights'll pull left. Don't blame the gun."
MABEL    "Bring it back before you break it and I'll only charge you once."
CONNIE   "I've had to redraw the third ring twice. It keeps being wrong. Thrilling."
PIP      "I could carry more than you. I've got a system."
WICK     "Mm. You'll want to be back before the second hour. Mm. Off you go."
```

### 12.2 Caravan note

Found in wrecks, 1 per 10 chunks. **The best worldbuilding space in the game.** 20–50 words. Written by
someone who did not know they were writing the last thing they would write, which means it should be
about something trivial. Never end on a scream.

```
Third time this month the eastern gate's been unmanned. I'm not filing it again.
If you're reading this and you're from the depot: the oil was short by two barrels
and I signed for four. Take it out of my rate. — H.
```

```
Tell Elsie the roof can wait. Tell her I said the roof can wait. She'll argue.
```

### 12.3 Almanac entry

18 → 20 enemy entries, 24 relics, 60 critters. Connie's voice: a short italic epigram, then two or
three flat sentences, then one detail she should not know. **Never a stat block.** The player already
knows the numbers from playing.

```
GLOAM
"The hole in the evening."
Not a knot of light but a knot of appetite. It does not need to reach you to
hurt you. Lead passes through and nothing happens.
Observed once at forty metres in good weather. The weather stopped being good.
```

### 12.4 Museum wing text

One paragraph per wing, unlocked by donation count. Wing 2's is Mabel's one scene (§8) and should be
**four sentences at most.** Restraint is the entire effect.

### 12.5 Obelisk record

The town square obelisk carves your records (GDD §3.4). Civic register, present tense, no adjectives.

```
DEEPEST LANTERN      1,840 m EAST
LONGEST HOLD         TIER 4, THREE WAVES
DARK REMOVED         6 PALLS
ROUNDS COMPLETED     41
```

## 13. THINGS DELIBERATELY LEFT OUT

So that nobody adds them thinking they were forgotten.

- **A creation myth.** Where the light originally came from is not a question this game asks.
- **A pantheon, a religion, a church.** The lamplighters had a *union*, at most.
- **A villain.** There is no one to defeat. There is a room to get to.
- **A chosen one.** You are on the rota. That is all you are and it is enough.
- **A written language nobody can read.** Everything in this world is written in plain hand on paper
  that is falling apart, and it is all mundane, and that is what makes it hurt.
- **Anything hostile with dialogue.** §11.1.5.
- **An explanation of the affinity system in the player's hearing.** §10.6.
- **A magic academy, a guild hall, a mentor, or a spellbook you find in a tomb.** Spells are a trade
  skill (§4.2). You learn them the way you learn to solder.
- **Military vocabulary.** §10.7.
- **A reason the city cannot simply be relit all at once.** It cannot because the grid is broken in
  hundreds of places and there is one of you. That is sufficient and it does not need a lock on it.

## 14. FOR THE RECORD

This file was written as a design document alongside `docs/design/MAGIC_KINETIC.md` and **contains no
code**. Every character, quotation, place name, material and beat referenced here is taken from the
existing GDD and extended rather than replaced.

**It does contradict `GAME_DESIGN.md` §1 in one place, deliberately, on instruction.** The GDD frames
the fiction as modern-military ("Call of Duty owns the 30 seconds"); `DIRECTION.md` §1 says the *feel*
stays and the *fiction* goes, and `DIRECTION.md` outranks the GDD by its own header. §10.7 is that
instruction written down. **The GDD should be amended and this file did not amend it** — see
`MAGIC_KINETIC.md` §14 #11, which records the same debt.

Five additions go beyond what the GDD states and are recorded as additions rather than smuggled in as
facts:

1. **Mabel's fingers have a cause** (a Husk, her brother). The GDD says only *"two fingers short on the
   left hand"*. This is an interpretation of an existing detail; if the owner prefers a different cause,
   nothing mechanical depends on it.
2. **Odo owns the Ashroot beds**, which makes the cosy farming system the oil supply chain
   (`MAGIC_KINETIC.md` §1.6). The GDD places Ashroot beds in town but does not name an owner.
3. **The Deepshade is the Wick's room**, which turns the Lv 30 gate from a biome unlock into an
   arrival. The GDD calls Deepshade *"the endgame biome"* and does not say what is in it.
4. **Magic is a trade with no mysticism in it** (§4.2). `DIRECTION.md` §2 asks for a spell school and
   says the lantern anchors it; it does not say what a spell *is*. This is my answer and it was chosen
   to delete subsystems — no wands, no mana, no gestures — rather than to add flavour.
5. **The city exists, is lit by the same grid the player repairs, and is why precision weapons are
   rationed** (§6a). `DIRECTION.md` §3 requires the city and the causality; everything else about the
   place is left open on purpose (§11.2.13).

**One thing this file deliberately does not do:** it does not name the city, place it, or describe
anyone in it. `DIRECTION.md` §3 makes it necessary; making it *specific* is a bigger decision than a
lore document should take on its own, and the fiction is more useful to the next writer as a stated hole
than as a guess I made.

## 15. §10 IS NOW ENFORCED — `docs/design/VOICE.md`

Added by the W7 anti-cringe pass, which the project owner asked for by name.

**§10 (REGISTER) has been right and unenforced for waves.** Somebody wrote six rules for how this
world talks and then had no way to make a HUD obey them, so the game shipped `THE GRID TAKES IT` —
a Proper Concept whose noun appears in **no other string in the game** — while this file was sitting
here saying *"civic, not epic"* and *"short sentences and ordinary words"*.

`VOICE.md` is that enforcement. It carries the full inventory of every string a player reads, a
KEEP/FIX/CUT verdict with a reason on each, and **a four-rule style guide** for the HUD specifically.
Read `VOICE.md` §5 before you write a line of on-screen text; read this file's §10 and §12 before you
write a line of anybody's *voice*. They are not the same job.

Three corrections to **this file**, found by that audit:

1. **The currency is SALVAGE, not Ember.** §6 and §1 imply Ember is what you spend. The code calls
   it Salvage and `src/sim/npc.hml` §7 gives the better reason: *"brass casings, wire, lamp glass —
   what a lamplighter brings home — and it is weightless, because a purse is not a load."*
   **Ember stays the substance** (§1: compressed light that sits in your palm as a coal). Salvage is
   what you sell, and it is the more civic word of the two, which is the whole register.

2. **EMBER HOLLOW is not in the game's own list of settlement names.** `client_site_name`'s sixteen
   are `LANTERNMOOR ASHFORD WICKHOLLOW GREYFEN EMBERCROSS THE FOLD COLDSTILE MARROWGATE SUNDER
   TALLOWDOWN BRIARLIGHT OLD KEEL HEARTHWAY NINE ELMS SALTMIRE THE LAST LAMP` — and §7 of this file
   is *titled* Ember Hollow. Nothing displays that table yet, so nothing is broken; but the day it
   does, the town this document is about will be called something else. `VOICE.md` §2.F22 has the
   verdicts on the other fifteen.

3. **§8's rule about Mabel was being broken by the code, not by a writer.** *"She tells you this
   exactly once… Do not soften her and do not give her a second scene about it. One scene. Then the
   weather."* — `client_talk_line` had exactly ONE daily line per villager and repeated it every
   in-game day forever, so the game told you about her fingers every day, and told you in a form that
   was **truncating on screen** (120 triangles against a 96-triangle clamp: it rendered as
   `TWO FINGERS SHORT, STILL STR`). Thirty daily lines now exist, six per person, in §12.1's form,
   and `src/sim/npc.hml`'s selector guarantees all six are said before any is repeated.

**And one thing this audit did not change, on purpose.** `THE DARK TOOK YOU` looks like a §10.2
violation — an abstract noun as the subject of a transitive verb, which is what capital-letter Evil
reads like. It survives because of §1 and §4: *light can be stolen, so there is something that steals
it*, and a drained creature *is not dead, it is empty.* **In this cosmology the dark taking your
light is not a metaphor, it is the axiom.** The sentence is mechanically literal, and that is the
test to apply to the next one: not "does it sound portentous", but "is it *true in this world's
physics*".
